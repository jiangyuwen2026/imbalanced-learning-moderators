Cover Letter for Pattern Recognition

Dear Editor-in-Chief and Reviewers,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Beyond Imbalance Ratio: Data Characteristics as Critical Moderators of Oversampling Method Selection" for consideration as a Research Paper in Pattern Recognition.

The prevailing IR-threshold paradigm suggests that datasets with higher imbalance ratios inherently warrant more sophisticated oversampling techniques. However, this assumption lacks rigorous causal validation. Our study challenges this paradigm through rigorous experimental methodology encompassing 12 controlled experiments (N>100 dataset variants) using 192 synthetic configurations and 18 real-world datasets from OpenML.

Our key findings include:
• Contrary to prevailing assumptions, imbalance ratio exhibits a statistically significant negative correlation (r=-0.68, p<0.001) with oversampling benefits when confounding data characteristics are controlled
• Class separability emerges as the strongest moderator (ρ=-0.72, p=0.003), substantially outweighing IR's influence
• We propose a "Context Matters" framework that provides evidence-based method selection criteria

State-of-the-Art Comparison:
Our comprehensive evaluation includes comparison with 7 representative oversampling methods spanning the evolution of the field: (1) Random Over-Sampling (ROS) - baseline method; (2) SMOTE (Chawla et al., 2002) - foundational method cited 15,000+ times; (3) Borderline-SMOTE (Han et al., 2005) - boundary-focused variant; (4) ADASYN (He et al., 2008) - adaptive synthetic sampling; (5) Safe-Level-SMOTE (Bunkhumpornpat et al., 2009) - safety-aware approach; (6) Geometric-SMOTE (Douzas & Bacao, 2019) - recent geometric variant; and (7) Tomek Links (Tomek, 1976) - data cleaning method. These methods represent the state-of-the-art from foundational techniques to recent advances, ensuring comprehensive coverage of the methodological landscape in imbalanced learning.

This work is particularly relevant to Pattern Recognition for several reasons:

1. Theoretical Contribution to Pattern Recognition: Class separability is a fundamental concept in pattern recognition. Our work establishes separability as a statistical moderator of method effectiveness, contributing to the theoretical understanding of data complexity in classification problems.

2. Methodological Innovation: We adopt controlled experimental design—widely recognized in statistical learning research—to isolate causal effects in imbalanced classification. This methodological rigor addresses limitations of observational studies prevalent in the field.

3. Connection to Pattern Recognition Literature: Our work builds upon foundational pattern recognition research on data complexity (Ho & Basu, 2002) and extends recent advances in imbalanced classification specifically published in Pattern Recognition (Koziarski, 2020; Zhang et al., 2019).

4. Practical Impact: The proposed framework enables practitioners to make evidence-based method selections grounded in measurable data characteristics rather than heuristics, directly supporting pattern classification applications in computer vision, medical diagnosis, and intelligent systems.

Our manuscript contains approximately 8,500 words, 46 references (including 6 papers from Pattern Recognition), 3 figures, and 5 tables. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission. We have no conflicts of interest to disclose.
